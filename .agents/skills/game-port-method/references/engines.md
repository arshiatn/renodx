# Engine notes outside Total War

Short facts per engine; Total War's (Warscape) are in `tw-port-checklist`. Each game's README and `HANDOFF.md` hold the details. The fact-sheet questions for a new engine: who owns jitter and render scale, depth direction, deferred or tiled lights, where the tone mapper and LUT sit, which settings are read live.

## Shader sources

- Offline dumps come from the game's unencrypted install packs, or from the maintainer's runtime dumps (`bin/cmd_Decompiler-1.3.16/dump_shader_<game>/`, not in the repo). Never decrypt protected assets or work around a protection (Denuvo, Steam DRM) to read them; code a protection hides is level B only through a runtime dump or an image the add-on saves of its own process (Ryse's and Tales' debug buttons).
- The decompiler traps in `tw-port-checklist`'s `shaders-ui.md` section 1 hold in every engine. More: 3Dmigoto misprints integer literals in compute shaders (Tales); `GatherGreen` came out as `Gather`, the red channel (Divinity 1's FXAA, wrong for every player at AA 1); `(int3)x & 0x7f800000` where the bytes do `asuint` / `asfloat` (Rome Remastered's bloom dither); sine and cosine swapped in a sprite rotation (Shogun 2's flares). Check such lines in the bytecode (`fxc /dumpbin`); the Vanilla-exact suite caught the last three ([hdr-review-rules.md](hdr-review-rules.md)). A constant printed short: the game's -pi (`l(0xc0490fdb)`) came out as -3.141593, which compiles one ulp off (360 sites in 139 files of Tales, Expeditions and ue-extended [c0594c1d1]). A gather4's channel and a final `and`: Rome Remastered's fxaa00 / fxaa01 read alpha / green where the decompile printed red, and their last `and r0.x, r0.x, <mask>` came out as an int cast of the float (WH2's fxaa00 had both) [9cc985336].
- A game that ships its shader sources may ship bytecode built from other values: read constants from the bytes. Divinity 2's orthographic EoCSight holds 50 / 100 nits in its bytecode against 240 / 300 in its source.

## Unreal Engine 4 (Expeditions: Rome 4.23, Tales of Arise 4.21)

Expeditions' exe says `++UE4+Release-4.23-CL-0` (no 4.24+ cvars such as `r.SkyAtmosphere`); older notes and comments call it 4.26. The "4.26" slots below are the slots read from that 4.23 exe: take a game's version from its exe's build string, never from a note.

- **Live graphics settings** are cvars written in memory (`talesofarise_dlss/engine_cvars.hpp`, one table for both games: 4.21 and 4.23 slots). Find `IConsoleManager::Get().FindConsoleVariable` by its call-site pattern (every site must agree on the singleton), call it by name from the present thread, and read each value's storage from its getter's code, never by calling it. Validate on `r.ScreenPercentage` (float 1..400).
- **4.21 against 4.23 slots**: `FindConsoleVariable` is vtable slot 0x90 in 4.21, 0xA0 with a third bool argument in Expeditions' 4.23; `AsVariableInt` / `AsVariableFloat` 7 / 8 against 9 / 10; `FConsoleVariableRef` `GetInt` / `GetFloat` 13 / 14 against 15 / 16. Read them from each exe, never copy them between versions.
- Writes skip UE's sinks: per-frame cvars apply live, others at the next area load. Values read only from UE's cached scalability copy (MaxResolution, MaxCSMResolution, ViewDistanceScale, StaticMeshLODDistanceScale, DetailMode, MaxAnisotropy) need `bCallAllConsoleVariableSinks` raised after the write. Raise-only (higher max, lower min), rewrite each frame if the game changes it, the game's value back on "Game's own" and unload.
- Crash traps: every sun cascade shares one CSM atlas (8192 at most; cascades that don't fit are silently not drawn); shadow resolution above 8192 or `r.Shadow.TexelsPerPixel` 4 written live crashed Tales. Bandai's "ScreenScale" cvars are integer divisors (lower is better): read the default from the registration code before choosing a direction. Matrices passed into UE functions must be 16-byte aligned.
- **Expand Gamut off in HDR modes**: zero the `ExpandGamut` term in the LUT builder (CombineLUTs) for every HDR mode, keep it only for SDR; it oversaturated both HDR modes in Expeditions.
- **Extended mode**: Expeditions' HDR Extended is a port of UE Extended's UE Filmic Extended (`src/games/expeditionsrome/ue_extended.hlsli`; source `src/games/ue-extended/`); start a UE game's Extended from it. Its shoulder above paper white rolls off per channel (`min(c, 1) + Neutwo(max(c - 1, 0), peak - 1)`), not by the brightest channel, or fire stays flat salmon (Ryse fix32).
- **The game's own contact shadows or SSGI** (Tales: Bandai's; Expeditions: UE's Contact Shadows): ours (Bend, GT-VBGI) replace them, never run beside them (no double work). Port them by feeding twattila's unchanged passes from UE's View constants (`talesofarise_dlss/ue_view.hlsli`: 4.23's rows are 4.21's, moved by 4 from row 8 on), with 1 - device Z as the depth and the sun from the engine's own shadow constants (Tales: Bandai's ScreenToShadow; Expeditions: the light's `FDeferredLightUniformStruct`). While ours run, the game's march toward the light and its GI term are skipped (Tales' contactshadow00 leaves out that march; its lighting passes drop only Bandai's GI add, never take the menu's GI-off path: that path's 0.001 stand-in halves the fill light's colour on faces turned from the sun, 46bb1c8a1). Before skipping the game's GI pass itself, census every reader with all bound slots (t16 and up): Tales' ten toon lighting passes read its output at t28-t30, so the chain keeps running. A replaced GI term can be much stronger than ours: Bandai's adds a lit surface's own light back (1.24x direct on one pixel), GT-VBGI only bounces, so open ground lost 33 % (runs2_1116); measure the look before the default. Check: `test/tw_ue_sss_gi` on WARP (contactshadow00 with Bend off equals the game's bytes, a control must differ). The rows stay off until an in-game look check, then go on by default.

## CryEngine (Ryse: Son of Rome)

- Shaders come packed in `ShaderCache.pak`: there is no offline dump to read before a run, so level B means "seen in a runtime dump" (`dump_shader_ryse`), and level A needs a trace of that run.
- The exe on disk is Steam-DRM encrypted: code offsets come from the image the DLSS add-on saves of its own process (`ryse_image.bin`), never from a decrypted exe.
- Cvars go through the game's console, past its whitelist (`rysesonofrome/graphics.hpp`).
- In-place format upgrades lost clamps here: every new add-on uses view cloning (`use_resource_cloning`, `src/mods/swapchain_v1.hpp`); don't retrofit an old one.

## D3D9 games (R.U.S.E.)

- **Patch the game's SM3 bytecode**, don't rewrite its shaders: insert a few instructions after a known read, every other token stays the game's. A generator batches a family's hundreds (R.U.S.E.: 642 object, 112 terrain and 120 water PS; 974 of 974 accepted).
- **Prove every patch offline**: on an SM3 interpreter, "off" (the neutral value) gives the game's output exactly and other values change it; then create every patched shader on a CPU-only D3D9 NULLREF device. The runtime refuses forms the assembler takes (an `lrp` into a temp that is also one of its sources).
- **Rays through a D3D11 draw replay** on the presentation proxy's D3D11 device: mirror the buffers at unmap, transpile each vs_3_0 to vs_5_0 with stream output (each proven against the SM3 VS interpreter), place static draws once by a placement key (shader, declaration, streams and their write counts, index range, model rotation, position registers; translation within a tolerance) and replay only skinned or moved ones (R.U.S.E.: 1,783 of 2,223 draws placed once). Capture camera-relative where world coordinates are large (about 3.8e5).
- **Shared textures**: D3D9Ex opens a D3D11-made shared texture only as RGBA16F, RGB10A2 or BGRA8 (R32F and RG16F gave E_INVALIDARG, so R.U.S.E.'s bridge never started). Let D3D9Ex create every shared texture and D3D11 open it; one written by compute gets a D3D11 work texture with the UAV, copied into the shared one before D3D9 reads it.
- **Reflection guides from the game's own shaders**: GI needs no G-buffer ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md)'s ambient route), ray-traced reflections need guides. A forward D3D9 game writes them as extra render targets from its patched shaders: 4 MRT with their own formats and write masks but one blend state, so each guide copies oC0's alpha; no MSAA, so guides only with the in-game AA off. Water that writes no depth gives its surface position through a guide.

## Divinity: Original Sin 2

- When a profile fails to load, the game creates `Debug_Client_Profile_N` and rewrites that profile's `profile.lsb`, `modsettings.lsx` and `InventorySettings.lsb`: back up every profile folder's files before a run, not only the active one's.
